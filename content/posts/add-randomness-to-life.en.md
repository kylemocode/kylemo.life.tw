"Why did you suddenly start learning electric guitar?" Quite a few friends have asked me this, puzzled.

To answer, I have to go back a year, to Chiang Mai, Thailand, in November 2025.

It was an ordinary day after finishing remote work. I was wandering around the Old City when the decor of a music bar caught my eye. The dim space was dotted with scattered warm lights, and as a traveler I felt a homey warmth I hadn't felt in a long time, so I decided to spend the night there.

In Taiwan, "Open Mic" is mostly associated with stand-up comedy, but in Chiang Mai, a paradise for jazz, it more often means improvised music. (Coming back this year, I noticed that most venues now use the term "Jam Session" instead.) That night I expected to see a local band performing after sweating through rehearsals, until the host asked: "Is anyone else up for performing?" Only then did I realize that everything that night was a random combination. Whoever could play drums came up as the drummer, whoever could play guitar came up as the guitarist, and it didn't matter that they didn't know each other. So the band lineup kept changing all night. Each group spent only about three minutes talking through which song to play and how to work together. The amazing thing is that, even though it was all so spontaneous, the performances didn't feel at all like strangers meeting for the first time without any rehearsal.

Besides giving it their all, the performers communicated through eye contact and smiles: whose turn it was for a solo, when the song should end. Before I knew it, the audience, swaying along to the melody and rhythm, were all smiling too, enjoying the moment. That kind of randomness gave me a stunning night.

Later, out of curiosity, I read up on it and learned that jazz musicians can hold a Jam Session (an improvised jam) because there is a shared musical framework everyone understands: a fixed song structure, chord progression, rhythm, and solo order. Musicians don't need to rehearse every section in advance. As long as they know these shared rules, they can improvise freely, respond to each other, and even create melodies that didn't exist before.

**"Enjoy freedom within a framework"** is how I interpret it, and this idea has had a major influence on my outlook on life.

"Why not add a little randomness to life?" That experience inspired me to start thinking this way.\
\
Maybe it's an occupational habit from being an engineer, but I've long been used to "think it through before acting" in everything from big to small: Which café should I work from today? Where should I eat today? Which friend should I meet today? What goals should I accomplish in my career this year? How much wealth should I have at what age... So many thoughts are circling in my head at every moment that "feeling the present" has become a hard experience to grasp.

I really like a line by the Danish philosopher Kierkegaard:

> Life can only be understood backwards; but it must be lived forwards.

In fact, at every moment when we have to make a decision, we can't have all the information. Regret or remorse usually comes from seeing the whole picture only when looking back afterwards. What-if questions like "if only I had... maybe I would have..." can bring imagination and creativity, but they can also leave us stuck in the mire of the past, unable to move forward.

Why not let things happen randomly once in a while?\
\
After that night I started making some random decisions: wandering the streets and picking a café that looked good, ordering a drink I normally wouldn't, accepting a spur-of-the-moment invitation from a new friend at the coliving space, or allowing myself to schedule nothing for a whole day to see what chance and my feelings in the moment would lead me to do.

These changes seem insignificant, but they slowly pulled me out of the loop of overthinking and anxiety. They also showed me that as long as I'm willing to leave a little blank space in life, it fills itself with unexpected inspiration and experiences.

Recently I picked dancing back up and started developing a new interest in electric guitar. What I find fascinating about both is probably that they have a framework to follow, yet enough freedom for each person to show their own unique personality and soul. My dance teacher once said she sees dance as a combination of "sport" and "art", but what exactly can be called art? I think randomness and variability are traits I value highly.\
\
That night in Chiang Mai, the randomly assembled live band made me feel that some of life's beautiful moments only appear when we put down the script and hold no expectations. Random encounters bring random experiences, and since it's random, it means there are infinite possibilities, and I firmly believe that's one of the reasons life is so wonderful.
